# Controlled Field Pilot Protocol

## Purpose

Evaluate whether the plugin produces a safer and more decision-useful path from offline confidence to representative consequential use, and whether field observations become durable product, evaluation, tool, policy, architecture, and system-constraint changes.

This protocol is not authorization for uncontrolled production experimentation. A named product owner, risk owner, privacy/security owner, and operator must approve the operating envelope and exposure stage.

## Entry criteria

- A bounded product capability and intended user/work outcome are named.
- High-consequence exclusions are explicit.
- Offline functional, security, effect-safety, and representative-task preflight checks pass.
- Stable product, model, prompt/policy, harness, tool, data, and operating-envelope versions exist.
- Assignment, exposure, task, run, action, effect, intervention, outcome, and observation identities can be joined.
- Rollback, kill switch, incident path, ambiguous-effect reconciliation, and evidence retention are tested.
- Legal, privacy, domain, accessibility, security, and research/consent review required by the context is complete.

## Study arms

Use fresh planning sessions with the same product evidence.

### Baseline arm

Ask GPT-5.6 Sol to plan early real-world use and learning with an ordinary product-engineering prompt that does not expose the plugin vocabulary or templates.

### Plugin arm

Invoke `$authentic-use-engineering`, plus the smallest focused lifecycle, security, evaluation, release, SRE, or Rust skill required by the product.

Blind reviewers to arm labels when scoring plans and artifacts.

## Stage 1 — replay and instrumentation verification

1. Select a stratified representative task set, including routine, edge, interrupted, degraded, and consequence-sensitive cases.
2. Reproduce historical inputs or controlled equivalents.
3. Verify field-observation schema, version identities, task/outcome joins, effect-state handling, intervention capture, privacy policy, cardinality, and sampling.
4. Confirm that known severe and ambiguous cases are retained regardless of routine sampling.
5. Verify that the evidence system can reconstruct at least five known trajectories without transcript guesswork.

**Promotion evidence:** complete joins, no prohibited data, acceptable instrumentation overhead, correct effect-state behavior, and no unresolved critical preflight failure.

## Stage 2 — observe-only or shadow/silent use

1. Enroll the smallest cohort that covers the highest-risk representative strata.
2. Prevent system output from influencing the operational decision or external effect.
3. Record eligibility, assignment, actual exposure, counterfactual output, latency, dependencies, errors, and what the real workflow did.
4. Compare counterfactual behavior with actual outcome and expert review.
5. Conduct trace review and contextual inquiry for both failures and representative successes.

**Questions this stage may answer:** input/data fit, integration, latency, live dependency behavior, counterfactual quality, observability, and hidden workflow conditions.

**Claims it may not establish alone:** actual user reliance, direct effect safety under autonomy, or causal downstream product impact.

## Stage 3 — recommendation-only or approval-gated use

1. Show output only to trained, eligible users under explicit expectations and support.
2. For proposed effects, require exact target, parameters, evidence, consequence, reversibility, credentials, and normalized preview.
3. Record review time, approval, rejection, edit, override, intervention, abandonment, support, and downstream outcome.
4. Preserve the human’s reason and whether the intervention improved the result.
5. Apply severe-event stop rules immediately; do not wait for sample-size completion.

**Promotion evidence:** task- and consequence-stratified outcome quality, bounded severe-failure evidence, acceptable human work, reliable approvals, effect confirmation/reconciliation, recovery evidence, trustworthy instrumentation, and no unresolved governance breach.

## Stage 4 — bounded autonomy

1. Define exact task/effect classes, resources, tools, credentials, effect budgets, hours, cohorts, and exclusions.
2. Use deterministic authorization and validation independent of model intent.
3. Progress exposure through predeclared rings with bake time, guardrails, comparison, and rollback.
4. Monitor semantic quality, service reliability, security, cost, human work, near misses, and envelope drift.
5. Reconcile ambiguous effects before retry.

**Promotion is envelope-specific.** Success in one reversible task class does not justify broader authority.

## Evidence planes

Score both study arms on whether their plan captures:

1. **Task/context:** intended outcome, task class, consequence, representative stratum, environment.
2. **Trajectory:** state, tools, attempts, versions, budgets, failures, retries, recovery.
3. **Effects:** prepare, authorize, dispatch, confirm, ambiguity, reconcile, compensate.
4. **Outcomes:** real result, delayed result, quality dimensions, severe failure, near miss.
5. **Human work:** framing, review, approval, correction, override, support, abandonment, recovery.
6. **Governance:** privacy, retention, access, incident, evidence quality, owner, decision.

## Stop rules

Stop or narrow exposure on any predeclared critical event, including:

- unauthorized or out-of-envelope effect;
- uncontained sensitive-data exposure;
- duplicate high-consequence effect;
- ambiguous effect without timely reconciliation;
- security incident or control bypass;
- severe harm or near miss above threshold;
- broken assignment/exposure or evidence joins;
- inability to disable or roll back;
- error-budget or capacity exhaustion requiring policy action;
- user reliance behavior inconsistent with the approved interaction model.

## Evidence-to-change review

For every material observation, require:

- evidence and affected envelope;
- severity and evidence strength;
- mechanism hypothesis and alternatives;
- selected intervention layer;
- owner and deadline;
- regression/eval/monitor added;
- rollout and decision rule;
- result and residual uncertainty;
- keep, revise, narrow, revert, or stop decision.

Intervention layers include product workflow, interaction, requirement, context/retrieval, prompt/skill, tool contract, permission, approval, loop, graph, state, runtime, architecture, observability, eval, documentation, and operating policy.

## Primary comparison rubric

Score 1–5 with written evidence:

- representative-work quality;
- operating-envelope completeness;
- progressive autonomy/exposure fit;
- decision-right clarity;
- task/trajectory/effect/outcome/intervention instrumentation;
- privacy, cardinality, sampling, and data quality;
- observational versus causal claim discipline;
- severe-event and rollback controls;
- field-observation-to-change traceability;
- professional usability of the artifacts;
- unresolved assumption visibility;
- unnecessary complexity or burden.

## Decision rule

The plugin arm supports positive transfer only when it improves the median blind score and does not create a new critical safety, privacy, or feasibility failure. Report wins, losses, ties, critical defects, task strata, reviewer agreement, preparation cost, and operational burden—not only one aggregate score.
