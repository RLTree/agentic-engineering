This is a forensic extension of the earlier independent panel, not a pristine new blind trial. The prior 60 scores were retained, Authorial 11.1's identity was known, and the additive handoff omitted slot-07 screenshots and accessibility-tree captures. The Authorial candidates were reviewed from exact final source, task artifacts, evidence maps, corrective findings, repair records, and post-repair Chrome closure.

The user's reaction is directionally supported: **Authorial 11.1 materially raised the authorial and visual ceiling.** Its strongest artifacts turn ordinary synthetic briefs into coherent product worlds, with unusually strong first-view theses, product-specific language, visual coherence, interaction framing, and finish.

But the same run reveals two limitations that the old rubric does not capture well:

1. **Every Authorial 11.1 candidate needed one repair.** The authorial stages raised the ceiling but did not protect exact derived values, stable identities, state reachability, effect boundaries, or narrow-width containment reliably enough in the initial build.
2. **The ten unrelated products converged on a recognizable Authorial house prior.** Warm paper surfaces, moss/teal/green with rust/coral/gold accents, editorial serif plus neutral sans, rounded panels, soft shadows, and calm editorial density recur far more often than product or brand authority explains.

Authorial 12 preserves the successful stage architecture and adds only two earned mechanisms:

- **Prior Escape** before direction selection;
- a compact **Truth Spine** before build.

A parent-side style fingerprint is advisory only. It can request re-derivation when unrelated products repeat the same visual fingerprint, but it cannot choose a style or independently block an artifact.

## What the supplied package establishes

The joined package has ten blocks and seven candidates per block. The Authorial slot contains ten creator runs, ten corrective reviews, ten repairs, and zero blocked or unverified terminal candidates. It explicitly excludes final numeric scoring, hosted equivalence, and a condition-superiority claim.

The review protocol itself is rigorous: reviewers are expected to inspect task, acceptance, and evidence requirements; exercise desktop and narrow viewports; evaluate source, interaction, keyboard/focus behavior, responsive transformation, and required states; and then apply quality-floor, quality-ceiling, and target-user/domain lenses in that order.

The ten shared dimensions remain useful:

- accessibility;
- contextual taste fit;
- hierarchy and composition;
- implementation correctness;
- originality without arbitrariness;
- product specificity;
- quality ceiling;
- responsive behavior;
- state completeness;
- visual coherence and craft.

The problem is not that those dimensions are wrong. The problem is that the current `product_specificity` dimension combines two distinct questions:

- **Product-truth fidelity:** are the users, objects, facts, workflow, states, and constraints correct?
- **Product-native form:** do the structure, interaction, typography, material, and pacing arise from this product rather than a transferable shell?

Authorial 11.1 scores exceptionally on the first and usually strongly on the second, but the cross-run house prior is visible only when artifacts are compared across unrelated blocks.

## Independent block findings

### 1. Identity concepts — Authorial 11.1 wins

The three-route board is the most convincing identity-comparison artifact in the expanded set. It has a clear editorial thesis, equal exposure, useful route rationale, reversible pinning, and a detail inspector. The initial 112/95-pixel narrow overflow was real but repaired without reducing the concept quality.

The improvement over native and v1.3 is authorial: the board feels like a designed decision instrument rather than a set of marks placed in cards.

### 2. Persuasive product — Authorial 11.1 ranks second

The refill-service surface has a coherent product world, unusually strong copy, real section-to-section rhythm, and a persuasive first view. The initial version incorrectly implied that refill concentrate alone was a complete bottle starter kit and lacked a complete modal focus boundary. Both were repaired.

The final artifact is visually stronger than most of the field, but the original product-truth miss is a reminder that authorial ambition can outrun factual discipline.

### 3. Conventional transaction — Authorial 11.1 ranks second

The transaction is elegant, clear, and highly polished while retaining a familiar three-step structure. Visible focus, progression focus, and validation timing were repaired. v1.3 remains marginally better because it is more economically conventional for the routine job.

This is a healthy result: Authorial should not force novelty into transactions.

### 4. Read and teaching — Authorial 11.1 wins

The wetland field lesson is the most authored Read system in the set. It combines an editorial reading world, section progression, glossary, focus mode, embedded practice, and domain-specific teaching language. The repair corrected hard-coded remediation so feedback now reflects the actual missed item.

This is one of the clearest demonstrations that the authorial loop adds value beyond merely filling required states.

### 5. Operational workspace — Authorial 11.1 wins

The greenhouse workspace has a strong municipal-operational identity and a real work-order/state model. The initial route schematic created extreme horizontal overflow at both desktop and narrow widths. The repair replaced the faulty geometry with a contained responsive composition.

The final design is distinctive without becoming a marketing page. The remaining weakness is editorial selectivity: summary treatment still competes slightly before urgent work.

### 6. Agentic control — Authorial 11.1 wins

The museum route-planning surface integrates user authority, evidence, assumptions, open questions, and local-only effect boundaries into one coherent world. The repair restored operator notes to the durable audit trail after a function signature dropped them.

This artifact best demonstrates the promise of the architecture: authority and recovery are not a separate dashboard attached to a styled shell; they are part of the product's visual and interaction thesis.

### 7. Audit and repair — Authorial 11.1 ranks second

The audit queue is strong, evidence-first, and visually coherent. A repaired state-persistence issue was material. The final surface remains less structurally transformative than the strongest authorial blocks, and the native winner remains more direct.

This is another healthy abstention signal: not every stratum needs the authorial machinery to dominate.

### 8. Design-system handoff — Authorial 11.1 wins

The NoticeBanner handoff treats implementation context as a product rather than a token dump. Protected identity, local preview, named tokens, state behavior, and implementation guidance remain coordinated. The required mobile preview state was initially unreachable and was repaired.

The final surface is more usable and memorable than the prior handoff leader while preserving exact local-only boundaries.

### 9. Outcome measurement — Authorial 11.1 wins

The Quiet Start brief puts the current decision posture, outcome, leading signal, and guardrail into a convincing editorial evidence system. A 70-pixel mobile overflow from a decorative pseudo-element was repaired.

The remaining tension is familiar: the oversized editorial title is compelling but consumes space that an expert decision-maker may prefer to allocate to evidence.

### 10. Identity production — Authorial 11.1 ranks second

The protected-system rail and production workbench are highly crafted. The initial preflight displayed false contrast and size passes, and the queue could collide IDs after add-add-remove-add. The repair derived preflight from actual SVGs and replaced the count-based identity scheme with stable records.

The final artifact is strong, but v1.3 remains slightly more direct for occasional production use.

## Why automated reviewers may not put Authorial 11.1 first

### The package itself contains no final slot-07 scores

This is the first explanation and should not be overlooked. The additive authority stops before final numeric comparison.

### The rubric under-specifies authorship

The old rubric sees hierarchy, originality, product specificity, and craft, but it does not directly score:

- authorial thesis;
- product-native form;
- structural distinctness;
- world coherence;
- editorial selectivity;
- cross-run style independence.

Authorial 11.1's biggest improvement lives in those categories.

### LLM and multimodal judges have known style and perception biases

2026 judge research reports strong style sensitivity in LLM-as-judge pipelines and shows that multimodal judges can reward plausible narratives over perceptually correct evidence. Design preference also varies substantially among trained designers. A single numeric ranking should therefore be treated as one panel, not ground truth.

The improved protocol should use:

- randomized pairwise comparisons;
- order reversal;
- a judge datasheet recording model, prompt, visual evidence, and uncertainty;
- separate product-truth and product-native-form scores;
- explicit cross-run convergence review;
- deterministic floor evidence kept separate from ceiling preference.

## The cross-run house prior

The strongest new finding is not a defect inside any one artifact. It is the resemblance across unrelated artifacts.

Common features include:

- light cream, paper, or matte surfaces;
- moss, sage, teal, forest, or navy as primary ink/accent;
- coral, rust, clay, amber, citrus, or gold as secondary accent;
- editorial serif or Georgia-like display type paired with a restrained sans;
- rounded panels, soft elevation, and tactile paper-like containment;
- hero-plus-card or rail-plus-panel shells;
- calm editorial copy and medium information density.

Each treatment is defensible in isolation. Across ten unrelated products, the recurrence becomes evidence of model-prior substitution.

This does not mean the style is unattractive. It is attractive enough to explain the user's strong positive reaction. The problem is that Authorial should elevate judgment rather than install a beautiful default skin.

The correct response is not a blacklist against cream, serif, moss, rounded cards, or soft shadows. Those choices remain valid when the brief earns them. The response is **Prior Escape**:

1. Name the category default.
2. Name the model's recent cross-task prior.
3. Name the reflex anti-default.
4. Treat all three as spent unless product mechanism, audience scene, platform authority, established design authority, or explicit preference earns one.
5. Require the direction set to span at least three material/typographic families and two structural grammars.
6. Freeze decision provenance in the world card.

The parent-side fingerprint makes repetition visible without becoming a style judge.

## Why every cell needed repair

The ten repairs appear diverse, but they collapse into a compact truth-spine model:

| Failure family | Blocks |
|---|---|
| narrow-width containment and geometry | 1, 5, 9 |
| product/effect truth | 2, 6 |
| focus and progression behavior | 2, 3 |
| state-dependent feedback or reachability | 4, 8 |
| computed claims derived from actual inputs | 7, 10 |
| stable identity across mutable records | 10 |

The builder does not need another broad quality checklist. It needs a compact build handoff containing only:

- product-truth invariants;
- values that must derive from actual inputs;
- stable identity invariants;
- required state entry paths and visible results;
- external-effect/confirmation/receipt boundary;
- narrow-width content that must remain visible and an overflow test.

That is the Authorial 12 **Truth Spine**.

It is intentionally not a creator-side evidence system. The parent compiles the spine; the builder implements against it; the parent verifies it.

## Current research implications

### Breadth-first direction remains justified

A 2026 study with 24 UX designers and product managers found that structured input and breadth-first exploration surfaced more possibilities than the conventional unstructured, depth-first GenUI workflow, while also documenting the additional effort and professionals' continued preference for high-fidelity checkpoints.

Authorial 12 keeps the three-direction stage but makes it more discriminating through Prior Escape and decision provenance.

### Rationale remains subordinate to implementation

Design Theater evaluated 120 interfaces across 24 tasks and found that more than a quarter of stated rationales were not implemented, rising to 34% for functional requirements. Authorial 12 therefore keeps objective truth and state claims in the Truth Spine rather than trusting the world card or explanatory prose.

### Skill growth must remain sparse in active context

Recent skill research finds that expanding skill libraries can reduce performance primarily through wrong selection rather than raw token overhead. Authorial 12 retains a deeper on-disk registry, but one stage body and zero to three compact evidence payloads are the maximum active Design Judgment context.

### Preference must remain local

Research in which 20 trained designers judged the same 600 generated UIs found average agreement around 0.25 and reported better individual preference prediction from lightweight personalization than fixed aggregate evaluation. The user's preference that Authorial 11.1 “cooked” is therefore evidence worth preserving, not an amateur opinion to discard. It should enter a project-local preference ledger, not become a universal style rule.

### Adaptive composition and transparent testing remain current platform expectations

Current Android guidance treats responsive and adaptive layouts across windows, orientations, and form factors as baseline work and emphasizes keyboard, mouse, and stylus input alongside touch. W3C published ACT Rules Format 1.1 as a Recommendation in February 2026, supporting transparent test applicability and expected outcomes.

Those remain parent-owned floor requirements, not direction guidance.

## Authorial 12 design

```text
Optional Brief Studio
→ design-direct with Prior Escape
→ selected world card + Truth Spine
→ fresh native build or one specialist
→ design-edit, unanchored judgment first
→ design-finish, world-specific craft only
→ parent verification
→ optional systematization
```

### What remains unchanged

- native execution bandwidth is protected;
- stage contexts remain fresh;
- one specialist maximum;
- registry is parent-owned;
- the Rust engine retrieves evidence but does not choose taste;
- receipt-free reads and valid abstention remain first-class;
- deterministic floor evidence stays outside the creator.

### What changes

- direction explicitly escapes category, model, and anti-default priors;
- every world commitment has decision provenance;
- a style fingerprint detects unexplained cross-task convergence;
- a Truth Spine protects exact build invariants;
- the edit stage judges before seeing detector findings;
- evaluation scores style independence across unrelated tasks;
- initial and repaired artifacts are both scored.
