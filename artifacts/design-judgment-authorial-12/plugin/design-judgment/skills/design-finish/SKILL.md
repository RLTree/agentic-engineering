# Design Finish

Finish the selected world. Do not redesign it.

## Protected boundary

Preserve the thesis, information architecture, product scope, salience map, structural grammar, and truth spine. Do not add features, a new visual language, or generic effects. Use the world card—not model habit or registry examples—as the source of material, typography, and motion character.

## Felt-craft pass

Improve only what the rendered artifact demonstrates needs work:

- typography, measure, line breaks, and hierarchy;
- spacing rhythm and alignment;
- copy specificity and recovery language;
- focus, selection, caret, underline, scrollbar, and browser-surface treatment;
- responsive re-composition and state continuity;
- loading, empty, error, conflict, success, and recovery finish;
- one earned product-specific detail at most.

## Interaction contract

For every meaningful interaction record:

- purpose and frequency;
- pointer, touch, keyboard, and assistive path where applicable;
- immediate feedback and completion state;
- abort, cancel, undo, or focus restoration;
- programmatic status communication;
- standard motion and reduced-motion behavior;
- whether motion is essential.

Frequent utility and keyboard actions should usually be immediate. State changes should be clear and interruptible. Explanation may be sequenced. Rare milestones may be expressive when the world earns it. “No authored motion” is a successful decision.

## Final judgment

Apply no more than five bounded changes and no more than six interaction contracts. Every change must strengthen an existing commitment or resolve an observed defect. If the requested improvement requires changing the thesis or structure, stop and return to `design-edit` with `rebuild` rather than smuggling a redesign into finish.

Return the changed regions, purpose, reduced-motion behavior, and evidence that should be rerendered. The parent owns objective verification and final delivery closure.
