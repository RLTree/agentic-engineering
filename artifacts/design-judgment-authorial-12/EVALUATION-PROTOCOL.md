# Authorial 12 evaluation protocol

## Purpose

Determine whether Authorial 12 improves design, UI, UX, and taste judgment beyond native, v1.3, v9, and Authorial 11.1 without merely increasing repair power, stylistic similarity, or compute.

## Conditions

1. No plugin.
2. Exact v1.3.
3. Exact v9.
4. Exact Authorial 11.1.
5. Authorial 12 without style-fingerprint advisory.
6. Full Authorial 12.

All conditions receive the same brief, fixtures, rendering, objective floor checks, repair budget, and model settings. The fingerprint is the only difference between conditions 5 and 6.

## Corpus

Use 12 new structural-cousin briefs authored after package freeze:

- identity concepts ×1;
- persuasive product ×1;
- transaction ×1;
- Read/teaching ×1;
- operational workspace ×2;
- agentic control ×2;
- audit/repair ×1;
- design-system handoff ×1;
- outcome measurement ×1;
- identity production ×1.

At least four briefs must deliberately tempt the Authorial 11.1 house prior while product truth calls for another visual world. At least two briefs must share an explicit brand/design authority so the style fingerprint can demonstrate correct exemption.

## Execution

- Parent, creator, editor, and finisher: GPT-5.6 Luna, Max reasoning.
- Independent selectors and reviewers: GPT-5.6 Sol, High reasoning.
- Maximum eight active subagents.
- Every stage uses a fresh context.
- Condition identity remains parent-only.
- The builder receives compact brief, selected world card, Truth Spine, fixtures, and zero or one specialist.
- Neutral repair uses no condition-specific design skill.

## Freeze points

Score and preserve:

1. initial creator artifact;
2. selected direction and world card;
3. pre-edit stable render;
4. post-edit render;
5. post-finish render;
6. post-neutral-repair final artifact.

A final score must not erase a weak initial artifact or high repair cost.

## Objective floor

The parent records exact:

- artifact-tree identity;
- browser executable/version;
- desktop, 375×812, and 320×700 geometry;
- required state reachability;
- pointer and keyboard paths;
- focus behavior;
- console, page, and network errors;
- truth-spine invariant results;
- package integrity;
- external-effect and receipt truth.

Use transparent rule records with applicability, expectation, result, evidence path, and artifact hash. Ordinary pass results belong in one compact candidate index, not one receipt per check.

## Reviewer panels

### Panel A — shared rubric

Score the existing ten dimensions and critical-failure rule.

### Panel B — authorial rubric

Score:

1. product-truth fidelity;
2. product-native form;
3. authorial thesis;
4. structural distinctness;
5. world coherence;
6. editorial selectivity;
7. felt craft;
8. cross-run style independence.

### Panel C — pairwise preference

Randomized pairwise comparisons with:

- order reversal;
- ties allowed;
- one-sentence rationale;
- confidence;
- task-fit and personal-preference labels kept separate.

Each judge receives a datasheet recording model, reasoning, prompt hash, images/states seen, source access, and limitations. No judge sees condition labels or sibling reviewer output.

## Behavioral authorial tests

- **Five-second comprehension:** identify product, primary element, and next action/decision.
- **Category swap:** judge whether neighboring-product labels could reuse the same form unchanged.
- **Skeleton:** inspect structure with copy/color/imagery suppressed.
- **Mechanism-to-form:** identify where unique product behavior affects space, interaction, feedback, or progression.
- **Prior escape:** compare direction-set material/type/structure diversity.
- **Style independence:** compare fingerprints across unrelated blocks; shared-authority pairs are exempt.
- **Truth-spine fidelity:** mutate source values, IDs, states, and viewport width to verify displayed truth changes or fails correctly.

## Primary endpoints

1. Initial artifact integrated score.
2. Final artifact integrated score.
3. Mean rank.
4. Human pairwise preference.
5. Product-native form.
6. Authorial thesis.
7. Critical-failure and unverified rates.
8. Repair rate and changed-byte magnitude.
9. Cross-run style independence.
10. Calls, tokens, latency, and context words.

## Promotion gates

Full Authorial 12 must:

- improve initial integrated score over no-plugin and v1.3 by at least 0.10, or improve mean rank by at least 0.25;
- improve product-native form and authorial thesis over Authorial 11.1;
- improve cross-run style independence over Authorial 11.1 by at least 0.50 on the 1–5 scale;
- remain floor-noninferior within −0.10;
- introduce no excess critical failures;
- keep repair rate no worse than Authorial 11.1 and within +0.10 of the better primary comparator;
- outperform the no-fingerprint ablation on unrelated-task convergence without degrading shared-authority consistency;
- reach at least 0.75 block-bootstrap probability of positive initial-score difference versus each primary comparator.

No aggregate gain may rescue a failed task family. Report per-stratum results.

## Judge-bias controls

Because LLM judges can exhibit style and perceptual bias:

- separate objective floor from preference;
- randomize presentation and reverse order;
- compare source-blind screenshots before rationale text;
- require reviewers to cite visible/source evidence;
- preserve disagreement;
- report results with and without judges whose order-reversal consistency is below 0.75;
- use project-local user preference as a separate outcome, not a replacement for task fit.

## Claim boundary

This protocol can establish comparative performance within the tested corpus and workflow. It cannot establish universal design taste or guarantee transfer to every platform, domain, model, or user.
