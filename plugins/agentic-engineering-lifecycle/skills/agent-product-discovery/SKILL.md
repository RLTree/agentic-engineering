# Agent Product Discovery

## Core principle

Discover the work before specifying the agent. Study representative users, tasks, constraints, workarounds, failures, incentives, and consequences; frame competing problem hypotheses; and make “do not build” a valid evidence-backed outcome.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the decision and stakeholders

State the investment decision, suspected problem, affected users and non-users, operators, approvers, downstream recipients, and people who bear failure costs. Record existing evidence separately from sponsor belief.

### 2. Sample representative work

Define the target task distribution by frequency, value, risk, variation, seasonality, data conditions, and edge cases. Recruit design partners or internal users who actually perform the work; avoid choosing only enthusiastic or technically fluent participants.

### 3. Observe the current system

Use interviews, contextual inquiry, artifact review, workflow mapping, support/incident data, and trace or log analysis. Capture triggers, handoffs, waiting, rework, exceptions, informal tools, decision criteria, trust, and hidden coordination.

### 4. Frame opportunity hypotheses

Express each opportunity as user/work outcome, current constraint, affected segment, proposed mechanism, expected value, potential harm, and disconfirming evidence. Separate a valuable problem from a preferred technical solution.

### 5. Assess agentic fit

Determine which parts need semantic judgment, adaptation, or generation; which are deterministic; where authority and effects sit; and whether a simpler workflow, information design, automation, or process change would solve the problem better.

### 6. Set evidence and stopping rules

Define what would justify concept validation, narrow the target, change the workflow, or stop. Include representativeness, data access, operational feasibility, adoption incentives, human-role implications, and safety or compliance constraints.

### 7. Synthesize a decision brief

Produce evidence-backed opportunity ranking, unresolved assumptions, next smallest research or field-learning step, and a recommendation to proceed, reframe, pause, or stop.

## Output contract

Return a **Product Discovery Evidence Brief** with:

1. decision, problem space, stakeholders, and current evidence;
2. representative work/task distribution and research sample;
3. current-state workflow, pain, workaround, handoff, and exception map;
4. opportunity hypotheses with value, harm, and disconfirming evidence;
5. agentic-fit and simpler-alternative assessment;
6. assumption/evidence map and evidence-quality limitations;
7. proceed, reframe, pause, or stop recommendation;
8. next smallest authentic-use or validation step.

## Common failures

- **solution-led discovery:** Interviewing users about a predetermined agent instead of understanding the work and alternatives.
- **enthusiast sample as market evidence:** Treating friendly design partners or power users as representative of the operating population.
- **pain point without decision context:** Cataloging complaints without the triggers, incentives, authority, consequences, and success criteria that shape the workflow.
