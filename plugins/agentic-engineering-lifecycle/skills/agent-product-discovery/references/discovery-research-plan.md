# Discovery research plan

Discovery establishes whether a real, consequential, recurring workflow problem exists and which outcomes matter before solution commitment. Agentic products require study of the whole human-tool-organization system, including current workarounds and decision authority.

## Vocabulary
- **problem evidence:** Observed costs, errors, delays, risks, unmet needs, or workarounds in a real workflow.
- **research question:** A falsifiable uncertainty about users, workflow, outcome, constraints, or adoption.
- **contextual inquiry:** Observation and questioning in the environment where work occurs.
- **non-user:** A relevant person who avoids, abandons, is excluded from, or is harmed by the current or proposed system.
- **opportunity boundary:** Conditions under which solving the problem would and would not create sufficient value.

## Decision procedure
1. State the product decision, existing evidence, assumptions, and what would cause stop/pivot.
2. Map stakeholders, roles, workflow stages, handoffs, incentives, current tools, consequences, and affected non-users.
3. Recruit across representative segments, including difficult, infrequent, accessibility, policy, and failure-prone cases.
4. Combine observation, interviews, artifact/log review, support/incident evidence, and baseline measures; avoid solution-leading questions.
5. Synthesize jobs/outcomes, friction, failure modes, constraints, current alternatives, and opportunity boundaries.
6. Record evidence strength, contradictions, missing segments, and the next discriminating validation.

## Decision table

| Question | Best starting method | Weak substitute |
|---|---|---|
| What work actually happens? | Contextual observation and artifacts | Stakeholder memory alone |
| How frequent/severe is the problem? | Operational/support data plus sampling | A loud anecdote |
| What outcome matters? | Observed consequences and user decisions | Feature preference ranking |
| Who is excluded or harmed? | Non-user and edge-segment research | Average-user persona |

## Evidence obligations
- Participants and tasks reflect the intended operating envelope, not only convenient enthusiasts.
- Separate user statement, observed behavior, researcher inference, and product proposal.
- Document existing alternatives and why people keep or abandon them.
- Discovery output contains a decision and uncertainty, not a feature backlog.

## Review questions
1. What consequential behavior did we observe rather than ask people to predict?
2. Which stakeholder bears cost or risk but is absent from the request?
3. What evidence would show this problem is not worth solving?
4. Is agent autonomy actually needed, or would workflow/tooling simplification solve the problem?

## Failure patterns
- Solution interview: presenting the concept and collecting agreement.
- Persona theater: fictional averages replace representative evidence.
- Feature mining: requests are accepted without tracing the underlying outcome or constraint.
