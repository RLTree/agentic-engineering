# Discovery evidence quality

Discovery evidence should alter investment decisions. Judge it by relevance, directness, representativeness, triangulation, provenance, recency, contradiction, and its ability to falsify the opportunity—not by interview count or presentation polish.

## Vocabulary
- **direct evidence:** Observation of the relevant workflow, behavior, consequence, or artifact rather than a report about it.
- **triangulation:** Convergence or productive disagreement across methods, sources, roles, and data.
- **saturation:** Point at which additional qualitative cases stop adding material mechanisms for the defined scope.
- **disconfirming evidence:** Observation that weakens, narrows, or falsifies the opportunity hypothesis.
- **evidence expiry:** Condition or date after which evidence must be refreshed.

## Decision procedure
1. For each claim, label observed fact, participant report, operational record, inference, assumption, or proposal.
2. Score relevance to decision, directness, population coverage, method quality, provenance, recency, and contradiction.
3. Triangulate workflow observations, interviews, artifacts/logs, support/incidents, baseline metrics, and non-user evidence.
4. Seek cases where the problem is absent, solved differently, or not worth changing.
5. Record confidence as bounded to segments/conditions; define next evidence and expiry.

## Decision table

| Evidence | Strength for workflow claim | Common limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Direct contextual observation | High | Observer/reactivity and small sample |
| Operational artifact/log | High for recorded behavior | Missing intent, unlogged work, instrumentation bias |
| Interview recall | Medium | Memory and social desirability |
| Survey preference | Low–medium | Prediction and self-selection |
| Internal opinion | Low | Role/incentive bias |

## Evidence obligations
- Contradictions are preserved, segmented, and explained rather than averaged away.
- Evidence quantity cannot compensate for poor relevance or representativeness.
- Discovery decisions include stop/pivot criteria and unresolved uncertainty.
- Agent summaries link back to raw evidence locations and distinguish quotation from inference.

## Review questions
1. Which claim rests only on self-report or internal belief?
2. What did non-users, abandoners, or harmed stakeholders reveal?
3. What evidence would cause us not to invest?
4. Has the workflow or market changed enough to expire the evidence?

## Failure patterns
- Consensus laundering: many similar interviews presented as triangulation.
- Deck confidence: polished synthesis hides weak provenance.
- Confirmation-only research: no negative case or alternative explanation sought.
