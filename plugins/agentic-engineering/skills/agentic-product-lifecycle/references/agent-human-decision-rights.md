# Agent–human decision rights

Decision rights prevent fluent automation from silently acquiring authority. Assign work by consequence, reversibility, evidence quality, and accountability—not by whether the agent can produce an answer.

## Vocabulary
- **decision right:** Authority to select or approve an option and accept its consequences.
- **execution right:** Permission to carry out a previously authorized action.
- **risk acceptance:** Named human approval of residual uncertainty or harm potential.
- **contestability:** Ability for an affected person to inspect, challenge, override, or appeal a decision.
- **approval fatigue:** Degradation of oversight when frequency, poor previews, or incentives turn review into routine clicking.

## Decision procedure
1. Inventory decisions and external effects across the lifecycle.
2. Classify consequence, reversibility, policy/value content, affected stakeholders, evidence quality, and time pressure.
3. Assign agent propose/analyze/execute permissions separately from human approve/accept/escalate duties.
4. Design review information, alternative presentation, uncertainty, time budget, expertise, two-person control, and emergency override.
5. Instrument approvals, edits, overrides, escalations, reversals, and sampled non-intervened successes; review for automation bias and fatigue.

## Decision table

| Decision type | Agent role | Human role |
|---|---|---|
| Reversible, policy-free, well-tested | Execute inside envelope | Set policy and monitor |
| High-impact but previewable | Prepare and explain | Approve or reject |
| Value/policy/stakeholder tradeoff | Synthesize options/evidence | Own decision and rationale |
| Irreversible or ambiguous effect | Do not autonomously execute | Authorize with recovery/closure evidence |

## Evidence obligations
- Authorization and execution are distinct controls.
- Affected users have an escalation or appeal path where appropriate.
- Approval UI exposes evidence, impact, alternatives, uncertainty, and rollback—not just an accept button.
- Low intervention is not automatically proof of quality; sample and inspect apparent successes.

## Review questions
1. Who is accountable when the recommendation is wrong?
2. Could the agent be accepting risk through a default, schema, or automatic transition?
3. Can the reviewer realistically inspect the decision at this volume?
4. What evidence shows oversight improves outcomes rather than moving liability?

## Failure patterns
- Agent-as-product-owner: value or policy choices hidden inside generation.
- Rubber-stamp approval: high frequency and weak previews make consent meaningless.
- No contestability: affected users cannot challenge or recover from a decision.
